# Method selection and bounded execution

Use this reference for a substantial or resumable task, not every small lookup.

State a compact brief: decision/question, requested output, material scope,
available prior work, access boundaries and what would count as a useful answer.
Split into units only where separate evidence or dependencies justify it.

Check early whether the evidence exists in the needed form. If a narrow exact
question cannot be answered, distinguish: not found in a bounded search;
inaccessible; absent from a supplied complete record; genuinely unsettled;
and requiring primary data. These are different outcomes.

For comparisons, write the inclusion criteria before averaging. Preserve unit,
entity, period, geography, denominator, sample origin and pay/price components.
A public asking price is not a transaction; an owner-selected anchor is not a
market estimate. Show adjacent evidence in a separate layer when useful.

For synthesis, count documents, distinct first-person voices, underlying studies
and independent observations separately as relevant. Recurring-author or
convenience corpora cannot establish field prevalence. Counterevidence may
change scope rather than require choosing a winner.

For tool failures record the attempted URL/path, time, method and raw failure.
Follow current harness/browser instructions. Do not treat a login barrier as
permission to bypass it, change models or route through another harness.

A run cap applies to the whole assigned work, including retries/child work when
used. Two failed attempts at the same blocker end that route. Do not manufacture
more search rounds to evade the cap. Save what was learned, and name the next
method that could actually resolve it.

For a checkpoint retain unit ID, done/unfinished state, current result/version,
source IDs and hashes where relevant, next permitted action and unresolved
limits. Do not republish completed work or overwrite raw evidence. A stale
summary cannot supersede a newer authoritative correction merely because it
was read last.

Useful pilot measures: decisive questions answered, eligible unique evidence,
material corrections, actions/retries, elapsed time and owner intervention.
Report monetary cost only from exposed reliable receipts; otherwise unavailable.
No source count or rubric score alone establishes usefulness.
